NAS 10

Subsequent events: adjustment, disclosure and going concern

The date of the event is only the starting point. The key question is what the event says about conditions at the reporting date.

By Rishab Dahal · Published and last reviewed 24 September 2026

Scope: General educational information. Entity-specific facts, materiality and authorization procedures require professional judgment.

Define the review window

Events after the reporting period are favourable or unfavourable events occurring between the reporting date and the date the financial statements are authorised for issue. An event outside that window belongs to a later reporting period, although other requirements may still apply.

Ask whether the condition existed at year-end

An adjusting event provides evidence about a condition that existed at the reporting date. The financial statements are adjusted to reflect that evidence. A later event that indicates a condition arose only after the reporting date is non-adjusting.

Do not stop at “non-adjusting”

A material non-adjusting event may require disclosure of its nature and an estimate of its financial effect, or a statement that such an estimate cannot be made. Materiality depends on both amount and nature.

Separate going concern from the ordinary sequence

If events after year-end show that the going-concern basis is no longer appropriate, the issue is more fundamental than a routine non-adjusting-event disclosure. The basis of preparation and related disclosures need reassessment.

A concise working sequence

  1. Record the reporting date, event date and authorization date.
  2. Describe the underlying condition and when it arose.
  3. Gather evidence linking the later event to the year-end condition.
  4. Conclude whether amounts require adjustment.
  5. If non-adjusting, assess materiality and disclosure.
  6. Reassess going concern separately.
